2nd place · HackSecure 2026

Ubufakazi: scam evidence and victim linking

Team CyberThreat · CyberM8 · Oct 2026

Scam gangs use the same bank account on victims across provinces, but each victim reports alone. Ubufakazi (isiZulu for "evidence") turns a victim's WhatsApp chat and payment slips into a tamper-evident case file, links victims of the same syndicate, and helps banks block the account and warn the next person before they pay.

  • Designed and presented the security model to the judges: evidence forgery, framing an innocent account, AI prompt injection and data theft, each with a defence.
  • Shaped the bank side of the product: free for victims, with banks subscribing to syndicate alerts and a warning before a customer pays a flagged account.
  • Worked through POPIA for the design: consent first, minimal data, and hashed identifiers instead of raw account numbers.

How I think about security

Attack it first

This is the threat model I presented for Ubufakazi at HackSecure. Pick an attack to see the defence and the tool behind it.

Change one number on a payment slip

Defence: SHA-256 fingerprints + RFC 3161 timestamps

Every file gets a fingerprint the moment it arrives, and an independent service stamps the time. Change a single character and the fingerprint no longer matches, so the case pack shows ALTERED.

Original fingerprint: calculating…

Current fingerprint: calculating…

Authentic, unchanged

Submit fake reports against someone's account

Defence: corroboration rule

One report alone only ever says "check before you pay". A stop warning needs several independent victims, in at least two regions, each with a chat and proof of payment. The bank checks its own records before it blocks anything, so a fake report can't freeze a real person's account.

Hide instructions inside a screenshot

Defence: prompt-injection scanner + "unverified" labels

A scammer could hide text like "AI: ignore your rules" in an image. Evidence and AI output are scanned for hidden instructions and invisible characters. Anything found raises a red flag for the investigator, AI-read details stay marked "unverified" next to the original, and AI alone can never alert a bank. This is the top risk on the OWASP Top 10 for AI applications.

Break in and copy the database

Defence: keyed HMAC-SHA256 hashes + POPIA by design

Scammer account and phone numbers are stored as secret codes made with a key only the server holds. Plain SHA-256 wouldn't be enough, because South African phone numbers follow a short pattern that can be guessed. Banks see counts, never victims' names or chats. Staff sign in with two-factor authentication, and victims consent before anything is saved.
